## Who can do this

- **Every household with a roof** can start rooftop harvesting.
- **The panchayat** can work on school and health centre roofs.
- **Women's groups** can be trained as water testers and caretakers.
- **MGNREGS or VB-G RAM G workers** build recharge structures and desilt ponds. The directory uses both scheme names, so check which applies now.

## What a roof can give

The directory's example: with **500 mm** of rain a year, a **100 m²** roof yields about **40,000 litres** a year. That roughly covers drinking and cooking for **4 to 5 people** in a household.

The sum is roof area × rainfall × a runoff factor.

- 100 m² × 0.5 m of rain = 50 cubic metres, or 50,000 litres, if every drop were caught.
- 40,000 litres is 80 percent of that. The rest is lost to splash, the first flush and leaks.

So the example works if a roof loses about one fifth of its rain. A rough, dusty roof loses more.

:::note
Annual rain is not daily water
The rain comes in a few monsoon weeks. A tank has to hold months of supply, so the tank size matters as much as the roof size.
:::
