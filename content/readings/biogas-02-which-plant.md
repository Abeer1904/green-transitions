Biogas plants can be sorted in two ways: **by how they are built** and **by what the gas is used for**.

## By how they are built

| Type | What it is | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed dome** (Deenbhandu model) | A brick-and-cement structure | Most common under the GOBARdhan scheme. Low maintenance. Can be small (household) or large (community). |
| **Floating drum** | An older design with a metal drum | Uses more metal, so it needs more maintenance than a fixed dome. |
| **Portable / prefab unit** | Ready-made, easy to move and pack | Easiest to install. Suits a single household. |

> A fixed dome is built from brick and cement, the same materials local masons already work with.

## By what the gas is used for

1. **Cooking gas only.** The simplest use. The gas goes to one kitchen, or a few.
2. **A piped village gas grid.** One plant, pipes to many homes. The directory names Naya Gaon, Hisar and Lambra Kangri.
3. **Electricity.** A biogas engine turns the gas into power.
4. **Compressed biogas (CBG).** The gas is compressed and sold as vehicle or industrial fuel. This is the same idea at a much bigger, more expensive scale, backed by national efforts like GOBARdhan.

## Do not mix them up

A household plant and an industrial CBG plant share a name but they are very different: different cost, different skills, different safety rules, different training. When you read about "biogas jobs", always ask *which kind of plant is this job in?*

:::ask
Talk about it together

- Would your village start with single homes, or with one shared plant?
- What would you need to agree on before sharing a plant?
:::

> The directory suggests a sensible order: **start with a household model, then combine into a small community plant if the amount of dung justifies it.**
