## The steps, in order

1. **Map the water together.** Walk the village with residents and mark ponds, wells, drains and places that waterlog.
2. **Bring in a technical partner.** A state rural development team, an NGO or an agricultural university extension team does the first proper site assessment.
3. **Build the earth structures.** Trained masons build the ponds, pits and recharge structures.
4. **Plan the work through the village plan.** Put it in the **GPDP** and use **MGNREGS** labour where it fits.
5. **Set up upkeep.** A maintenance rota and a record book, agreed before the structure is handed over.

## What it costs

The directory gives these ranges. They are guides, not quotes.

- **Site assessment and technical partner:** ₹15,000 to ₹30,000.
- **Small farm pond:** ₹35,000. The directory gives one figure and no size.
- **Soak pit:** ₹10,000 to ₹15,000.
- **House rooftop rainwater harvesting:** ₹15,000 to ₹30,000.
- **Borewell recharge:** ₹16,000 to ₹50,000.

:::note
Check before you budget
The directory gives no source or year for these figures, and no cost for restoring a johad. Get local quotes and ask the technical partner what is included.
:::

:::ask
Talk about it together

- Which structure would help the most households for the least money?
- What would a record book need to hold for the next person to find it useful?
:::
