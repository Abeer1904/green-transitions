## What they looked at

The Global Water Partnership wrote this paper before the UN Rio+20 summit in 2012. It asks how water fits into a green economy: growth that does not use up the natural resources it depends on. The paper says water is not only part of the economy, it is inside it. Without water, farming, industry and energy stop.

## What they found

- **Demand is rising against supply.** The paper cites an estimate that, at current rates of use, demand will exceed supply by as much as 40% by 2030.
- **Water and growth go together.** Citing a 2005 WHO source, the paper says poor countries with better access to clean water grew 3.7% a year on average. Countries of the same income without it grew 0.1%.
- **Farming is the big user.** Agriculture uses as much as 90% of water in some countries, so gains in farm water use help other sectors too.
- **Many countries plan for it.** 84 of 133 countries in a UN survey have plans for Integrated Water Resources Management. The approach balances efficiency, fairness between users and protection of nature.
- **Storage is one answer.** The paper calls for rethinking storage, from rainwater harvesting and natural storage up to large reservoirs and aquifers.

## What it means for a village like Malai

The paper has nothing on India, Haryana or groundwater pumping. Its message is about whole systems: water is used by many people, and it often has no single body in charge. It says water is easily exploited and polluted because management is weak.

For Malai, that turns to local questions. Who uses the water, and who decides? The paper also lists skills development among the needs of a green transition, but it names no trades or courses. The training routes in the earlier water reading remain the guide.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **Method and sample:** A policy argument. It cites figures from other sources and reports no survey or experiment of its own.
- **Where and when:** Global, written in June 2012. Examples include sub-Saharan Africa and South Africa.
- **How strong the evidence is:** This is a policy report from a global water network, not a study with its own data. Its figures are quoted from other reports. The paper itself says it is unclear whether water investment drives growth or growth drives water investment.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** No village-level or Indian data. The 40% and 90% figures are global or country-specific and say nothing about Malai's tubewells.
:::
