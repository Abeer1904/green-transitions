## What they looked at

Researchers asked farmers what pushes them to use renewable energy on the farm, mainly solar irrigation pumps. They also asked whether using it goes with better farm output and steadier money. They surveyed **251 farmers** in rural Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and the rural belt around Delhi NCR.

## What they found

- **Community ties counted most.** Farmers who reported more community involvement, peer influence and cooperative activity reported more use of renewables. This was the strongest link in the model (0.430).
- **Government support counted too.** Subsidies and incentives went with more adoption (0.307).
- **Adoption went with better farms.** Farmers who reported more adoption also reported higher productivity (correlation 0.589). Higher productivity went with better financial stability (correlation 0.636).
- **Community life did not lift productivity directly.** The direct link from social participation to productivity was zero (-0.0003). In the model, its effect ran through adoption.
- **The survey named four barriers:** high upfront cost, limited access to finance, lack of technical knowledge, and maintenance and operating problems.

## What it means for a village like Malai

Farmers in the study adopted more where neighbours shared what worked. A village with a cooperative or a farmers' group has that in place.

Two of the four barriers are about skills, not money: technical knowledge and maintenance. The authors call for technical knowledge programmes. In a village, that means local people who can install, service and repair.

The study did not separate grid-fed tubewells from off-grid pumps. It does not say which fits Malai.

:::facilitator
For facilitators
- **Method and sample:** Questionnaire survey, 288 responses collected, 251 kept after screening. Statements rated on a 5-point scale. Analysed with structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM, SmartPLS). Small and medium farmers who used renewables or knew of them.
- **Where and when:** Rural Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh, Delhi NCR rural belt. Dates not given.
- **How strong the evidence is:** Weak to moderate. The survey records what farmers agree with, not measured yields or income. Many statements are worded as positive claims, such as "Government subsidies encourage the adoption of renewable energy." The links are associations, not proof of cause.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** The paper's numbers conflict in places. It gives the adoption to productivity link as 0.373 in one paragraph and 0.627 in the table. It reports a positive coefficient for barriers (0.148) while writing that barriers hold adoption back, so the barrier result is not used here. The authors name the single-time design and limited geography as limits. No results are given for Palwal.
:::
