## What they looked at

Two researchers wrote a policy brief on India's irrigation pump market: which pumps farmers buy, what subsidies pay for, and what this does to groundwater. They interviewed **26 farmers and pump dealers** in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh and added national data.

## What they found

- **Most pumps run on diesel or grid power.** About **9 million diesel and 21 million electric** pumps are in use. Diesel rose from Rs 38.5 a litre in 2010 to Rs 89 in 2024, and farmers moved to electric.
- **Electric pumps drew down groundwater.** The brief names Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. In the northwest, only **18% to 25% of wells** are classed as safe for extraction.
- **Solar does not replace diesel by itself.** Diesel pumps are not scrapped when a solar pump is sold. Farmers with solar pumps still use diesel pumps.
- **Solar is costly for a small farm.** A 5 hp solar pump in Uttar Pradesh costs Rs 2,73,137. The government pays Rs 1,63,882. The farmer pays Rs 1,09,255, against an average farm household income of Rs 8,000 a month.
- **Repair support is thin.** Named problems: lower flow than diesel pumps, poor panels, no after-sales support.
- **Renting is common.** In Samastipur, Bihar, a diesel pump rents at Rs 160 an hour and an electric pump at Rs 80 to 90.

## What it means for a village like Malai

Haryana is one of the states where electric pumps drew groundwater down. Malai's grid-fed tubewells sit in that picture. A solar pump has no running cost for water, so the brief says farmers have no reason to pump less. Solar alone does not protect the water table.

The brief also points to a skills gap: after-sales repair. A trained village technician fills it. It also calls for metered tubewells and better local groundwater data.

:::facilitator
For facilitators
- **Method and sample:** Policy brief. 26 semi-structured interviews with irrigating farmers (diesel and electric pump owners) and irrigation dealers, plus secondary data such as IEA pump counts and government figures.
- **Where and when:** Interviews in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh. Published June 2024. Some sources date from 2021 and 2022.
- **How strong the evidence is:** Moderate for the national picture, weak for detail. The brief gives no interview counts by group and no quotes.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** No interviews in Haryana. The authors advise caution, since states differ. The brief says PM-KUSUM gives a 60% subsidy with 30% from loans and 10% from the farmer, but its own Uttar Pradesh example has the farmer paying 40%. The solar-05 reading gives 30% each from Centre and State. Check with HAREDA before committing money.
:::
