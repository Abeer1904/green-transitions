## What they looked at

Soil organic carbon is the carbon held in soil as decaying plant and animal matter. The authors say more of it improves fertility, water retention and yield stability. The authors gathered 147 published field studies from across India, covering 1,020 comparisons between a regenerative practice and a plain control. They asked which practices raise soil carbon, and by how much.

## What they found

- **Overall, soil carbon rose.** The authors put the average gain at about 0.17 percentage points, or 17% above the control fields.
- **Farmyard manure led among the common practices.** On the paper's scale, manure scored 8.92, green manure 6.56, compost 5.07, crop residue retention 5.15 and fertiliser management 5.06.
- **Less tillage alone scored lower.** Zero tillage scored 0.98 in studies of 10 to 20 years. The authors say it needs organic inputs alongside it.
- **Time matters.** Gains were larger in long-running studies, and the authors describe a fast start that slows later.
- **No single practice works everywhere.** Results differed by region. Where water limits plant growth, manure and green manure worked especially well.

## What it means for a village like Malai

A village with cattle already has farmyard manure. The paper points to manure and green manure as consistent ways to build soil carbon, and to residues kept on the field.

The gains build over years. The paper says smallholders often need returns quickly and recommends support during the transition. It names composting training through self-help groups as one tool. It did not test biogas slurry.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **Method and sample:** A meta-analysis. It pooled 147 studies and 1,020 data pairs from 2000 to 2024, using a random-effects model. Effects are shown on a unit-free scale, so read them as a ranking.
- **Where and when:** All India, in many farm regions. The Trans-Gangetic Plain had the most data, 181 comparisons.
- **How strong the evidence is:** A large pooled review, but results vary a lot between studies. After a correction for missing negative results, the overall score fell from 3.46 to 1.98 and stayed positive. Where studies lacked figures, the authors filled gaps with estimates. The paper gives the share of long-term studies as 15% in a table and 40% in the text.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** The paper reports no result for Haryana. Biochar scored highest, 14.4, but came from only 10 studies, mostly short-term, with a range that includes zero. It measured soil carbon, not yield or income.
:::
