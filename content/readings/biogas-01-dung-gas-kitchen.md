## What is bioenergy?

Every day, a village makes waste: cattle dung, kitchen scraps, leftover crop stalks, poultry litter. **Bioenergy** turns this waste into something useful.

Inside a sealed tank, the waste breaks down without air. It gives off a gas rich in methane. This is **biogas**, and you can cook on it. What is left over is **slurry**, a wet manure that the fields can use.

So one process gives you two things: **gas for the kitchen** and **manure for the field**.

## Why it matters in a village like Malai

In villages like Malai or Banchari, many households still cook on a *chulha*. It burns wood or dung cakes.

Look at what is already true:

- Villages have a lot of cattle.
- Cattle make dung **every single day**.
- Most of that dung is not put to work as fuel or a business.

The directory calls this a locally sourced raw material that is "produced daily yet underused".

## A health reason too

Smoke from a chulha stays in the kitchen. It affects **women and young children** most, because they spend the most time near the fire. Cooking with biogas removes that smoke. That is why the directory treats biogas as a health measure as well as an energy one.

:::ask
Talk about it together

- Who cooks in your household, and what do they burn?
- Who collects the fuel? How much of their day does it take?
- Where does the dung go at the moment?
:::

## The idea to hold on to

Biogas is not a machine you buy. It is a **loop**: cattle, dung, plant, gas, kitchen, and slurry going back to the field. Every part of the loop needs someone with the right skill. The rest of this track is about that loop and the people who keep it turning.
