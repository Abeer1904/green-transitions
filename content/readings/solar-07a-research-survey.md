## Why read this

The tracks in this site rest on the skills directory. This reading sets that directory beside eleven studies and reports. It shows where they agree, and where nobody has looked yet. Each study has its own short reading, listed at the end.

## Four findings that repeat

**Uptake followed cash, assets and connections.** In India's national survey, biogas owners had higher income, more cattle, electricity and a toilet, and SC and ST households were less likely to own a plant. In Kerala, homes without rooftop solar liked the idea but named cost, payback and information as the barriers. Among 251 farmers in north India, community involvement went with more renewable use. In these studies, interest was not the barrier. Money, information and neighbours were.

**Trained is not the same as employed.** The Suryamitra programme trained 48,157 people by FY20. Placement fell, few earned much and few were women. In Nairobi, solar jobs sat in sales, and sealed kits shut out local repair technicians. Training numbers do not match the jobs that exist.

**Repair and upkeep are the gap.** The Nairobi study, the pump brief and the farmer survey all name maintenance and after-sales support as a weak point. The Kerala study recommends training local technicians for installation and maintenance.

**Who gets in matters as much as what is taught.** The green skills framework argues that women are held back less by missing skills than by social norms and missing openings such as credit, travel and family consent.

## Around the energy

Energy choices sit inside a household and a landscape. The Bibipur survey (Jind, 2004) found 98% of households cooking on biomass and families asking for LPG and electricity first. The Mysuru study linked long years of wood cooking to more COPD, cough and airway inflammation in women. The pump brief finds that cheap power pushes groundwater use up, so a solar pump does not protect the water table by itself. The soil study finds that manure and green manure raise soil carbon across India, and gains build over years.

## What the research does not tell you

- **Almost nothing is from Haryana.** Bibipur is a single village surveyed in 2004. The farmer survey covers Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh and Delhi NCR but reports no separate results.
- **No study tests solar on grid-fed tubewells**, the case in Malai.
- **No study follows the skills of a village biogas operator.** The adoption survey names the daily work but does not study who does it.
- **Most evidence is a snapshot.** The surveys show association, not cause. Several papers contain figures that conflict with themselves, and the facilitator box in each reading says where.
- **Subsidy figures differ between sources.** Check with HAREDA or the DISCOM before anyone commits money.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **How this survey was made:** Each paper was read in full and summarised in its own reading. Every figure here comes from those readings. Where a paper's numbers conflict, the reading says so.
- **Evidence strength:** The set mixes surveys, a meta-analysis, a conceptual review, two policy papers and a programme report. No two use the same method, so the findings are compared as themes, not pooled.
- **What to do with it:** Use the four repeated findings as prompts. Ask the group which one it sees in its own village.
:::
