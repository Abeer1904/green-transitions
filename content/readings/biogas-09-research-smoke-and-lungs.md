## What they looked at

Doctors in Mysuru compared two groups of women. All were 40 or older, had never smoked and were the main cook at home. 903 rural women cooked only on wood. 441 periurban women cooked only on LPG. The team used questionnaires, breathing tests and a breath test for airway inflammation.

## What they found

- **COPD was more common among wood users.** COPD is a long-term lung condition that narrows the airways. 3.90% of wood users had it, against 1.59% of LPG users. In the 40 to 69 age band the figures were 3.41% and 0.7%.
- **Cough and bronchitis were more common.** Cough: 44.5% of wood users, 30.2% of LPG users. Chronic bronchitis: 2.55% and 0%.
- **The breath test for airway inflammation was higher.** A high reading was found in 20.3% of wood users and 8.1% of LPG users.
- **More years and hours at the stove went with more problems.** The authors multiplied years of cooking by hours per day. Chronic bronchitis was linked to a score of 60 or more, COPD to a score of 90 or more. Lung function measures fell as the score rose.
- **Some things did not differ.** Wheezing was similar in both groups. So was a pattern of low lung volume (37.5% and 35.8%).

## What it means for a village like Malai

The study did not test biogas. It compared wood with LPG. It shows that long daily hours cooking on wood went with more lung problems in these women.

The paper also reports that 20% of beneficiaries of India's LPG programme do not use their connection, and that beneficiaries take on average three free refills a year. It treats fewer than eight cylinders a year as continued reliance on biomass. A connection is not the same as daily use.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **Method and sample:** Cross-sectional study, 1,344 women. Breathing tests, a breath test for airway inflammation, and chest scans for a subset of 69 women.
- **Where and when:** Mysuru, Karnataka. Women were tested between 2019 and 2023.
- **How strong the evidence is:** Objective clinical measures and a clear dose pattern. Exposure was estimated from years and hours of cooking, not measured in the air. The paper has only 35 COPD cases among wood users and 7 among LPG users. The scan results carry no statistics.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** The two groups differed in education and standard of living (80.7% of wood users had a low standard of living, against 20.4% of LPG users). The authors name this as a limit. Dung cakes were excluded, and the paper does not cover Haryana.
:::
