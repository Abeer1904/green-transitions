## What they looked at

Why do a few rural households own a biogas plant while most do not? The authors used a national survey, the India Human Development Survey. They studied 10,384 rural households. Only 167 of them owned a plant.

## What they found

- **Owners were better off and had more cattle.** Average monthly household income was ₹10,912 for owners and ₹3,768 for non-owners. Owners had 5.36 cattle on average, non-owners 2.82.
- **Owners had electricity and toilets.** Electricity: 92.2% of owners, 68.5% of non-owners. A toilet with a tank: 60.7% of owners, 26.7% of non-owners.
- **SC and ST households were left behind.** They were 10.7% of owners and 27.7% of non-owners. In the statistical model they were less likely to own a plant.
- **Families with many children and few adults were less likely to own one.** The authors link this to labour: a plant needs regular feeding and cleaning.
- **Education and awareness alone did not predict ownership.**

## What it means for a village like Malai

Ownership follows cattle, cash and services. In Malai, ask who has cattle, a toilet and steady power, and who does not.

The paper quotes NABARD figures for a small plant: ₹16,250 for equipment, against a maximum subsidy of ₹15,000. The authors say banks hesitate to lend without collateral. They recommend microfinance, insurance for the cattle of poor families, and community plants where households with no land or cows give labour instead.

The paper also lists the daily work: mix dung and water in a 4:5 ratio, spread the slurry, clean the plant, and check for clogging, rusting and leaks. This is the work of a plant operator.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **Method and sample:** Logistic regression on India Human Development Survey data. 10,384 rural households, 167 owners. Two estimation methods gave almost identical results.
- **Where and when:** All India, 33 states, 1,503 villages in the full survey. The data were released in 2008, which the authors call not recent.
- **How strong the evidence is:** Large and nationally representative, but a single snapshot. It shows who owns plants, not why. Effects are reported as small changes in probability because ownership is rare. The paper gives the sample as 10,384 in one place and 10,348 in another. The text calls income significant, but the results table shows no significance mark for it.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** Haryana is not reported separately. The cattle count covers cows, bullocks and draft cattle, and does not name buffaloes. Costs and the subsidy figure are older NABARD numbers.
:::
