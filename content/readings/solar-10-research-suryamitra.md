## What they looked at

The government's Suryamitra programme, started in 2015, trains people for solar installation and maintenance. The course is 600 hours, about three months, residential and free. By FY20 it had trained 48,157 people. The researchers asked whether graduates find and keep solar jobs.

## What they found

- **Placement fell.** Training partners say 70-75% of graduates get an offer. Government training data shows an average of 51% from FY16 to FY20, falling from 61% in FY16 to 31% in FY21.
- **Pay and contracts are weak.** Only 18% of Suryamitras are on a company payroll. In FY16 about 70% earned under INR 10,000 a month. Only 4% were still in solar after three years.
- **Few women.** Women were 2% of trainees in FY16 and nearly 3% in FY17. The long residential course and remote postings are named as reasons.
- **Training and jobs do not match.** In FY20 Karnataka needed 1,019 workers for construction and maintenance but trained 711. Uttar Pradesh needed 67 and trained 1,831.
- **Practice is thin.** One training partner's kit supports up to 2 kW, while employers mostly hire for MW plants.

## What it means for a village like Malai

- A certificate does not guarantee work. Jobs depend on where solar is being built.
- A chart in the report shows Haryana at about 1,285 trained across FY16 to FY20 (read off the chart, not stated in the text).
- Many left because jobs were out of state, on contract and poorly paid. Local work is the gap.
- The authors recommend a stream for solar entrepreneurs, shorter or hybrid training, and job fairs with local companies.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **Method and sample:** Mixed methods. Interviews and online forms with 7 industry, 6 training-partner and 15 Suryamitra respondents, plus analysis of the government training body's database and annual reports.
- **Where and when:** India, all states, FY16 to FY20 data. Report published September 2022.
- **How strong the evidence is:** The counts are large but the database is, in the authors' words, poorly maintained and incomplete on placements. The interview sample is small and picked by convenience.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** Interviewees were mostly Hindi and Marathi speakers. Industry response was low. The study did not follow Haryana graduates. Wage data is for FY16 only.
:::
