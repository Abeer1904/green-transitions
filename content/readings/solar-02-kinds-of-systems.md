## By what they are for

1. **Household rooftop solar (RTS).** On a home or shop roof. Powers appliances directly.
2. **Ground-mounted systems.** On a large plot of land. Used for big farms, solar parks, businesses or feeding the grid.
3. **Agricultural solar water pumps.** For irrigation, in place of diesel pumps.

## By how they connect

1. **Grid-tied.** Connected to the local electricity grid. Extra power goes back to the grid through **net metering**: the extra units become a **credit on your bill**. Rates vary from state to state. Best for homes that already have a grid connection.
2. **Off-grid (independent).** Stores power in batteries and runs on them. Best for remote places that are hard to reach.
3. **Hybrid.** Connected to the grid *and* has a battery. When the grid goes out, it switches to the battery automatically.

## Which one for which need?

| If the problem is… | Look at… |
|---|---|
| Rising electricity bills | Grid-tied rooftop |
| Cuts and outages at home | Hybrid (has a battery) |
| Diesel cost for irrigation | Solar pump |
| A place the grid does not reach | Off-grid |

:::note
Backup is a design choice
"Solar" does not automatically mean "power during a cut". A grid-tied system is built around the grid. It is the battery in a hybrid or off-grid system that carries you through an outage. Always tell the installer what you need to keep running.
:::

:::ask
Talk about it together

- What must stay on when the power goes: fans, lights, a phone, a pump, a fridge?
- Does everyone in your village have a grid connection?
:::
