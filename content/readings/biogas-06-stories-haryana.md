## Naya Gaon, Hisar: a shared plant

The community built a **400 m³ biogas plant** under the GOBARdhan scheme. It sends gas by pipe to **150 households**.

*What to notice:* this is the "piped village grid" idea from the second reading, working at village scale.

## Dhani Mahu, Bhiwani: land first

The sarpanch **donated land** near the gaushala for a biogas plant.

*What to notice:* a plant needs a site, and the site needs someone with the power to give it. Sometimes the first decision is not about technology at all.

## Panipat: crop waste with a price

Indian Oil's Panipat plant converts farm waste into fuel. It **buys rice straw directly from farmers**, and the plant makes about 30 million litres of ethanol a year. Once residue has a price, it becomes a commodity. That means **a village aggregator can earn from collecting it.**

## Kaithal: a plant on the gas grid

The Kaithal compressed biogas plant, developed by Demeter Agro Energies with Indraprastha Gas Limited, **buys paddy straw at fixed rates, typically ₹800–₹1,200 per tonne**. The gas is compressed and fed into the city gas grid.

## A plug-flow design

A plant by Arciplug in Haryana uses rice straw together with cow dung, using a plug-flow design to raise the gas output.

:::warn
Different things, not copies
Panipat and Kaithal are industrial-scale plants, not village plants. They show that a **market for feedstock exists**, and that aggregator work can pay. They are not a model to copy at Malai's scale.
:::
