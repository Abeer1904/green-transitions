## What they looked at

Kenya has pushed solar hard, and policy promises local jobs and less waste. The researchers asked who repairs broken solar equipment in Nairobi, and what happens to it afterwards.

## What they found

- **Jobs are in selling, not fixing.** In 2017-2018, renewables and off-grid energy in Kenya had 25,000 direct jobs. Sale and distribution made up 40%. Operation and maintenance made up 5%.
- **Big companies repair in one place.** Most repairs happen in workshops in Nairobi. Even in Mombasa, the second city, a PV specialist is hard to find.
- **Local technicians are shut out.** The self-employed technicians, called fundis, already ran a repair trade in Nairobi. Companies prefer to replace rather than repair, and place little faith in fundis' skills.
- **Kits are built to stay closed.** Some kits have company-specific screws, and circuits coated in paint so part numbers cannot be read. Only the maker's spare parts fit.
- **Repair is turning into replacement.** For inverters, repair mostly means swapping the controller card. Only the glass of solar panels is fully recycled. There is no local recycling for lithium batteries.

## What it means for a village like Malai

- The study says most repair requests are for inverters and controllers. It names overheating from dust, animals and insects, and installation that does not match the installed capacity.
- Batteries and panels are not repairable.
- Warranties often fail because buyers have no proof of purchase date. Keep the bill.
- Before buying, ask: can a local technician open it, and are spare parts available nearby?
- The study did not look at grid-fed tubewell pumps.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **Method and sample:** Qualitative field survey of about thirty semi-structured interviews with technicians, staff of the main solar companies, regulators and informal electronics repairers. The paper adds policy documents and reported statistics.
- **Where and when:** Nairobi, Kenya, January and February 2020. Published 2022 as part of the Hybridelec research project.
- **How strong the evidence is:** Interview-based, with no count of repairs or of fundis. The job shares come from a census the authors cite. The claim that reuse markets exist for solar is left open by the authors.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** The market studied is mostly pay-as-you-go home kits and Kenyan rules. Malai is on the grid. The Kenyan company practices described here are not evidence of what sellers do in Haryana.
:::
