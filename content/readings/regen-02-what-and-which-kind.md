Regenerative or natural farming cuts or removes bought chemical fertiliser and pesticide. It uses what a village already has to rebuild soil: cow dung, cow urine, jaggery, pulse flour, mulch, cover crops and less tillage. The directory calls one version **Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF)**.

## Why it matters in an agrarian village

For a village growing wheat, mustard, bajra, cotton, jowar and moong, dung, slurry and compost can stand in for bought inputs. The directory says this also eases pressure on groundwater that is already stressed.

## Four approaches

- **Natural farming or ZBNF.** Every input is biological and made at home. Nothing is bought and no synthetic input is used.
- **Organic farming.** Inputs come from off the farm, so it does not run at zero cost.
- **Mixed cropping.** Two or more crops are grown together on the same land, with seeds mixed before sowing. This helps keep certain nutrients in the soil.
- **Conservation agriculture.** Soil is disturbed as little as possible and machines pass less often. At least 30 percent of crop residue stays on the surface.

## Two scales

- **A smallholder cluster or collective.** Farmers pool resources. The directory's example is one cow that can serve input preparation for 30 acres.
- **An FPO or cooperative brand.** Farmers sell together under one name.
