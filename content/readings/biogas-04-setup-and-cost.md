## The steps, in order

1. **Find your feedstock.** Survey the dairy households and the gaushala. Count cattle. Estimate how much dung can actually be collected.
2. **Register.** Apply for **GOBARdhan** for a community or CBG plant, or the household biogas programme for family units.
3. **Build.** Engage a trained rural mason to install the first plant.
4. **Hire and train.** An operator, an aggregator, a slurry processor and a compliance recorder.
5. **Pipe it (if community).** For a village-wide supply, lay pipelines with a meter and a small user fee.
6. **Link the slurry to the fields.** Agree who takes it and how.

Notice that *survey comes before registration, and registration before construction.* Most trouble starts when people skip ahead.

## What it costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Cattle and dung survey + detailed project report | ₹10,000 |
| GOBARdhan registration | Free, through the portal |
| Household plant: mason + construction | ₹2,000–₹2,300 (as given in the directory) |
| Community plant (45–400 m³) | ₹45 lakh – ₹91 lakh |
| Operators, while in training | ₹15,000 in total |
| Operators, on a formal salary | ₹25,000–₹40,000 |
| Village-wide piped supply | ₹15,000 per household |
| Slurry-to-field arrangement | ₹1–₹3 lakh |

:::note
Read these as a guide
These are the directory's figures. Prices change and schemes change. The household plant figure looks low compared with the community figure. Ask a qualified constructor for a current quote and a written scope.
:::

:::ask
Talk about it together

- Which costs would be met by a scheme, which by households, and which by the group?
- Who would look after the money?
:::
