## What they looked at

The researchers surveyed Bibipur village in Jind district, Haryana, in 2004. The village has 445 houses, 2,670 people and 2,225 animals. They counted the energy the village has and the energy it uses, for homes, farms, transport and small industry.

## What they found

- **Cooking used the most household energy.** It took 52.51% of all domestic energy. About 98% of households cooked on biomass: crop residue, cow dung and firewood.
- **Cooking fuels, as shares of domestic energy:** firewood 20.21%, cow dung 19.5%, LPG 7.96%, biogas 2.98%. Across all household uses, biogas supplied 5.91%.
- **Efficiency differs a lot.** The paper gives thermal efficiency as 9 to 10% for a chullah burning straw, 35% for biogas and 85% for LPG.
- **Views on biogas were split.** 35.8% of families liked it very much, 23.4% liked it and 40.8% did not like it. LPG: 80.8% liked it very much. Asked what they would choose if money were given, 42.5% said LPG and 50.5% said electricity.
- **On farms, irrigation took the largest share of energy:** 41.7%, followed by tilling at 35%.

## What it means for a village like Malai

Bibipur is a Haryana village, so the picture is close to home. Dung and firewood together supplied about 40% of domestic energy there. The paper says the village's biomass has not been used properly. It recommends introducing biogas technology only after weighing the other factors.

The paper's own table shows biogas available at 10,780 MJ against 2,900 MJ used. It does not explain the gap. Families in Bibipur said they wanted LPG and electricity first. A plant in Malai has to compete with a fuel people already ask for.

The paper does not cover skills, costs or plant design.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **Method and sample:** Door-to-door survey. The paper says 500 households and, elsewhere, 445 houses. Energy converted to megajoules with standard factors.
- **Where and when:** Bibipur, Jind, Haryana. Surveyed December 2004.
- **How strong the evidence is:** A descriptive case study of one village. No comparison with other villages and no statistical tests. The paper has some inconsistencies: cooking is 52.1% in the abstract and 52.51% in the table, and the per-capita figure of 20.02 MJ a day does not match the village totals. Check figures before quoting.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** Data are over 20 years old. One village, so it does not stand for Haryana or for Malai. The paper does not say how many households had a biogas plant, or what plants cost.
:::

- Which farm work in your village uses the most energy: irrigation, tilling or something else?
:::
