## What is it?

**Rainwater harvesting** catches rain where it falls, to use directly or to refill the groundwater. **Watershed management** looks after the whole catchment: ponds, drains, bunds, check dams, plants and soil, so the land holds water and the aquifer recharges.

The directory calls this a **high-impact sector** for rural areas, since a severe drinking water crisis affects about 30–35 villages.

## Three levels

- **Household.** One home stores rainwater and cleans it for basic uses.
- **Panchayat or community.** Traditionally a **johad**, a dam that captures and stores monsoon rain. Johads fall under the water-security priority of the Vikshit Bharat Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin).
- **Multi-village watershed.** Under the PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (Watershed Development Component 2.0), farmers, landowners and the whole village benefit.

## Skills the directory names

- **Rooftop rainwater harvesting mason or plumber.** Designs, builds and maintains storage tanks, filter chambers and recharge pits. Needs to understand roof slope, runoff and how to fit PVC downpipes and gutters. Gives masons and construction workers income in dry seasons. Training routes: **Rural Mason Training** (Ministry of Rural Development) and Haryana Skill Development Mission courses at Swaranjayanti Kaushal Vikas Kendras.
- **Water quality tester.** Collects and tests samples for chemical, physical and biological contaminants. Training: **NIFTEM, Sonipat**; **PHED Haryana** (field testing kits); and an **Agriculture Skill Council of India** qualification for Soil and Water Testing Lab Technician. Courses cost roughly **₹3,000–₹7,000.**
- **Pond / johad survey and rejuvenation supervisor.** Oversees restoring damaged water bodies from start to finish. The directory gives no training details yet.
