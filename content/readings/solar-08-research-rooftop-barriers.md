## What they looked at

Two researchers interviewed 52 households in Kerala between January and March 2024. None of the households had rooftop solar. They asked two things: what do you think of rooftop solar, and what stops you from installing it?

## What they found

- **Most people liked the idea.** 75% of coded answers about first thoughts were moderately or very positive (21 of 28).
- **Money came up most.** The researchers counted how often each barrier was mentioned: financial 55, information 41, technical 37, regulatory and policy 19, social 9, psychological 8.
- **Cost and payback.** The authors conclude that high upfront cost and doubt about return on investment were the biggest deterrents.
- **The roof itself.** Technical problems included too little roof space and poor roof orientation.
- **Not enough information.** Households lacked good information. The authors say current ways of giving it are inadequate.

## What it means for a village like Malai

- Interest was not the problem in this study. Cost, payback and information were.
- Before a household signs up, ask for the full cost and the payback time in writing.
- Check the roof first: space, shade and direction.
- The authors recommend training local technicians and electricians for installation and maintenance. That is a skill a village can hold.
- The study looked at home roofs. It did not test tubewell pumps.

:::facilitator
For facilitators

- **Method and sample:** Semi-structured face-to-face interviews with 52 households that had not adopted solar, chosen on purpose. Coded in NVivo 14 for themes and sentiment. Of the 52, 36 lived in panchayat areas, 26 in rural areas, 47 in villas, 34 were men and 18 women, and 37 held a master's degree or higher.
- **Where and when:** Kerala, January to March 2024. Published in Clean Energy, 2024.
- **How strong the evidence is:** Small qualitative study. The counts are mentions of a theme, not numbers of households. There is no statistical testing. The authors checked their reading with peers and shared summaries with some participants.
- **Limits and what does not transfer to Haryana:** The sample is small and highly educated. The authors warn against applying the results to other regions. Kerala has its own policies and grid. Farmers and irrigation were not studied.
:::
